# R-layer Mode Theory (RLMT)
An independent research program in theoretical cosmology by Tsuyoshi Tohi.
Since July 2026 its predictions are tested against cosmological data, and every
result is reported, including negative results and corrections.

---

## 🔷 Current status (November 2026)
Since July 2026 the theory is tested against data. The 2026 papers fit RLMT's
predictions to Planck, ACT, SPT, DESI, Pantheon+ and DES supernovae with public likelihoods, and
report negative results and corrections in full. Where they conflict with an
earlier Volume, the 2026 papers take precedence (see *Archive* below).

**What the data say so far**
- **Primordial spectrum (RLMT-lite).** Planck 2018 alone: Δχ² = +0.9, ΔAIC = +10.9.
  ACT DR6 with Planck (P-ACT-lite): Δχ² = −4.4, ΔAIC = +5.6. ΛCDM is preferred
  on grounds of economy; the data neither exclude nor require the three-mode mixture.
- **Spectrum from Lindblad dynamics.** In its minimal form (the Ω layer outside the
  inflationary clock) RLMT predicts a featureless power law. With one further coefficient μ
  (not fixed by the theory) it predicts a small feature in the CMB damping tail. Built with the mean sound
  speed, the model with μ = 1 and the weak-coupling exponent ν = 0.3 is consistent with
  P-ACT-lite (Δχ² = −1.7 to −4.0, depending on how sharply relaxation starts; global
  significance about 1.3σ after the look-elsewhere effect), but the feature
  is too small to be detected even by the Simons Observatory (≈ 1.6σ).
- **Non-Gaussianity.** f_NL = 0 when each mode is produced with sound speed c_s = 1; with the
  coefficient μ, a small negative equilateral f_NL (about −0.2 to −0.3 for μ = 1). Not testable at present.
- **Tensors.** RLMT does not predict r at present (the direct horizon-exit reading was rejected).
- **Hubble tension.** No RLMT model tested so far moves H0 toward the local values.
  Late-time Ω-layer dark-energy models fit as well as w0wa but leave
  H0 at about 68–69.5 km/s/Mpc; their gain comes from the shape
  "w = −1 today, phantom in the past", reproduced by non-RLMT control models, not from the layer structure.
  No redshift, direction or local-void dependence of H0 is seen inside the supernova data at the level required.
- **What H0 = 73 would need.** Only a component before recombination that shortens the sound horizon
  by about 6.5% reaches it at low cost. Early dark energy with its onset at matter–radiation equality
  and a scale-invariant spectrum (n_s = 1) gives H0 = 73.3 ± 0.5 without SH0ES, but raises S8 to 0.84–0.85,
  a cost of 1.8σ to 4.5σ depending on the weak-lensing survey. With n_s left free the same model gives H0 = 70.1 ± 1.1.
  It is not an RLMT model. A varying electron mass does not reproduce this: with n_s = 1 it costs Δχ² ≈ +71.
- **Early modified gravity.** The conformally coupled Layer-2 scalar is exactly dark radiation (no-go).
- **Cosmological constant.** Lindblad dissipation of the Ω layer does not give the observed Λ
  (unimodular mechanism: zero with an internal bath, far too large with the de Sitter horizon as the bath).
- **The Ω layer as a dark sector.** Realised as an electroweak-scale U(1)_T sector, it gives an inelastic
  dark-matter benchmark, m_χ = 277 ± 8 GeV with a 56.6 GeV dark photon and dark Higgs, allowed by current
  indirect-detection, cosmological and collider limits with margins of 2.5 to 10.5. A larger SU(3)
  representation for the layer fixes no new number (negative result).
- **Shortcuts through the R-direction.** Constrained by GW170817 to be of no practical use.
- **Numerical tools.** The hi_class audit found and fixed several problems in the code; they have been
  reported to the CLASS and hi_class developers.

## 🗄 Archive (theory-only Volumes, March–July 2026)
RLMT was first written down on March 16, 2026. The Volumes from that period are
kept as the record of how the theory developed. They are theory-only and were
not fitted to data. Claims in them that the papers from July 2026 onward
(titled "RLMT: ...") test and replace include the H0 triplet of Vol. XLI and
the layer-dependent expansion rates of Vol. XLI-L.
